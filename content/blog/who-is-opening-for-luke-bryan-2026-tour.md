[Luke Bryan](/artists/luke-bryan)'s **Word On The Street Tour 2026** doesn't have one opening act. It has a bench. Unlike tours that carry the same support bill coast to coast, Bryan rotates a pool of rising country artists in and out by market, usually three per night. That means the answer to "who is opening for Luke Bryan" genuinely depends on which show you're going to. Here's the full breakdown.

## The Opener Pool

Across the 2026 routing, these are the names showing up on Word On The Street bills:

- **Karley Scott Collins**: One of the most frequent names on the tour, an emotive vocalist and writer on the rise
- **Shane Profitt**: Tennessee traditionalist with a blue-collar streak, known for "How It Oughta Be"
- **Zach John King**: Smooth-voiced newcomer who broke through via viral covers before signing in Nashville
- **Drew Baldridge**: The "She's Somebody's Daughter" singer, a veteran of the independent grind
- **Lauren Watkins**: Sharp-penned Nashville writer with a classic country lean
- **Lanie Gardner**: Powerhouse vocalist who went viral covering Fleetwood Mac before pivoting to her own material
- **Randall King**: Texas honky-tonk revivalist, the most traditional-sounding act in the pool
- **Jake Worthington**: Texas neo-traditionalist with a George Strait-school baritone
- **RaeLynn**: The most established name in the rotation, joining the Alpine Valley show

Exact dates and tickets are on the [Luke Bryan artist page](/artists/luke-bryan).

## Farm Tour and the Double Down Stadium Shows

Two other flavors of Luke Bryan show are on the 2026 calendar:

- **Farm Tour (September)**: Bryan's annual tradition returns with four farm shows in Latrobe (PA), Millersport (OH), Brookston (IN), and Allegan (MI). These are standalone events on working farms, separate from the arena tour, and they historically feature their own support bills.
- **Double Down Tour with [Jason Aldean](/artists/jason-aldean)**: Co-headline stadium dates including two nights in Missoula in early August, Petco Park in San Diego, Nationals Park in Washington, DC, and a two-night December finale at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas. Two full headline sets, no traditional opener needed.

There's also a **Luke Bryan & Friends** night at Nashville's Bridgestone Arena in September, which is traditionally a guest-heavy one-off.

## What to Expect

Bryan's headline set is a two-hour party: "Country Girl (Shake It for Me)," "Play It Again," "That's My Kind of Night," and enough crowd work to remind you he's spent a decade hosting award shows. The rotating openers keep the undercard fresh, and with three support acts most nights, gates-to-encore runs long. Get there early: the opener pool is stacked with artists likely to be headlining their own tours in a few years, which has always been the Luke Bryan tour formula.
