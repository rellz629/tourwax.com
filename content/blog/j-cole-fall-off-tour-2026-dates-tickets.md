[J. Cole](/artists/j-cole) is on the road for what he's calling the last one. The Fall-Off Tour supports *The Fall-Off*, released February 6, 2026 and billed as his seventh and final studio album, and the routing matches the stakes: 73 shows across North America, Europe, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa, running from July 10 through December 12. It's his first solo headlining arena tour in five years and his first global run since 2017.

If you've been waiting to see Cole live, this is the tour to do it. Here's everything you need to know.

## The Shape of the Tour

The tour opened with two hometown-state nights in Charlotte in mid-July and works through more than 40 North American shows before closing the leg in Fayetteville, North Carolina in late September. That's not an accident: Fayetteville is where Cole grew up, and ending the American run at the Crown Coliseum is the kind of full-circle gesture the whole tour is built around.

From there it's 20 shows across Europe in October and November, a seven-show Australia and New Zealand leg from late November into early December, and a finale at FNB Stadium in Johannesburg, South Africa on December 12: an 87,000-capacity stadium closer for a tour that's otherwise all arenas.

## Who Is Opening for J. Cole?

No support acts have been announced for The Fall-Off Tour, and shows on the current leg have run without a traditional opener. That said, Cole's history and the album's guest list have fans expecting surprise appearances in major markets, and Dreamville artists are always candidates to pop out at select stops. If openers are added for the European or Oceania legs, the [J. Cole page on TourWax](/artists/j-cole) will reflect it in the listings.

## Europe, Oceania & the Johannesburg Finale

The European leg opens in Berlin on October 7 and runs through Oslo on November 12, with stops including Zurich, Amsterdam, Antwerp, Paris, Dublin, Birmingham, Glasgow, Manchester, Nottingham, Hamburg, and multiple nights at London's O2 Arena. The Oceania leg covers Brisbane, Sydney, and Auckland from November 25 through December 6, and the tour ends at Johannesburg's FNB Stadium on December 12.

## Tickets: What to Expect

This is a farewell-framed tour by one of the biggest rappers of his generation, so demand is heavy in every market. A few things that help:

1. **Check multiple nights in multi-show cities.** Second and third nights consistently have better availability and cheaper resale.
2. **Watch resale the week of the show.** Prices in most markets soften 7 to 10 days out as sellers move inventory.
3. **Consider the smaller-market plays.** Dates like San Antonio and Sacramento tend to price below the coastal metros for the same production.

Current prices and direct ticket links for every show are on the [J. Cole page on TourWax](/artists/j-cole).

## What the Show Looks Like

*The Fall-Off* is a dual-disc album built around Cole's life at 29 and at 39, and the show leans into that structure: it plays like a career retrospective as much as an album tour, moving through the *Forest Hills Drive* and *KOD* eras alongside the new material. If this really is the last album and the last full tour, it's being staged like a legacy statement, and the setlists reflect a catalog spanning 15 years.
