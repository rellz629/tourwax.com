Concert ticket prices have been climbing for years, but there are still plenty of ways to see live music without emptying your wallet. Whether you're chasing a stadium headliner or a club show, these strategies will help you find the best deals.

## Compare Prices Across Platforms

The single most effective way to save money is to compare prices. The same ticket can vary by 20-40% between platforms. [TourWax](/artists) pulls listings from both Ticketmaster and SeatGeek so you can compare prices side by side before buying.

Don't just check primary sellers either. Resale marketplaces sometimes have tickets below face value, especially for shows that haven't sold out.

## Buy Tickets Early (or Very Late)

There are two sweet spots for ticket prices:

**Early bird pricing**: Many artists and promoters offer lower prices during initial on-sale windows. Presale access through fan clubs, credit cards, or venue memberships often comes with better pricing tiers.

**Last-minute deals**: If a show hasn't sold out, prices on resale platforms tend to drop in the 48 hours before the event. Sellers want to recoup something rather than eat the cost of unused tickets. Check [concerts tonight](/concerts/tonight) or [shows this weekend](/concerts/this-weekend) for last-minute options near you.

## Target Weeknight Shows

Friday and Saturday shows command premium prices. The same artist playing a Tuesday or Wednesday show will often have significantly cheaper tickets. If your schedule allows it, midweek concerts are one of the easiest ways to save.

Many artists play multiple nights in the same city. The first and last nights of a residency or multi-night run tend to be pricier than the middle dates.

## Look Beyond the Big Venues

Smaller venues almost always mean cheaper tickets. An artist playing a 2,000-capacity theater will charge less than the same artist at a 20,000-seat arena. Plus, the experience is often better — you're closer to the stage and the sound is more intimate.

Browse [concert venues](/venues) to find smaller rooms in your area. Many mid-size venues host touring acts at a fraction of arena prices.

## Use Presale Codes

Most major tours offer presale windows before general on-sale. Here's where to find codes:

- **Artist fan clubs and newsletters**: Sign up for mailing lists of artists you want to see
- **Credit card presales**: American Express, Chase, and Citi all offer early access to select shows
- **Venue presales**: Follow your local venues on social media — they often share codes for their presales
- **Spotify and Live Nation**: Spotify sends presale codes to top listeners, and Live Nation has its own presale tier

## Consider Festival Passes

If you want to see multiple artists, festival passes often offer better value per act than individual concert tickets. A weekend festival pass that costs the same as one arena ticket might let you see dozens of artists.

Check [upcoming festivals](/festivals) for multi-artist events near you. Many festivals offer early-bird pricing months in advance.

## Set Price Alerts

Some resale platforms let you set alerts when tickets drop below a certain price. If you're flexible on timing, this passive approach can land you deals without constantly checking.

On TourWax, you can browse all [upcoming concerts by city](/concerts) to spot shows with competitive pricing from multiple ticket sources.

## Skip the VIP Packages

VIP and premium packages can be 3-5x the price of standard tickets. Unless the extras (meet-and-greet, merch bundles, premium seating) are genuinely worth it to you, standard admission gets you the same music.

Many venues have general admission sections where the experience is just as good. Standing-room floors at smaller venues often have better sightlines than seated sections at larger ones.

## Travel to Cheaper Markets

Ticket prices vary significantly by city. A show in New York or Los Angeles will almost always cost more than the same tour stop in a mid-size market. If you're willing to drive a few hours, you can often save more than the cost of gas.

Browse [concerts by state](/concerts) to find nearby shows in less expensive markets. Our [most toured cities](/insights/most-toured-cities) rankings show which cities have the most options.

## The Bottom Line

The best strategy is to plan ahead, compare prices, and stay flexible. Use tools like [TourWax](/) to track tour announcements early, compare ticket prices across platforms, and find shows at venues and dates that fit your budget. Live music is worth every penny — but there's no reason to overpay.
