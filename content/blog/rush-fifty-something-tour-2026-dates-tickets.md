Rush added **four new North American arena shows** to the Fifty Something Tour on August 31, 2026: **Houston on October 1, St. Louis on October 21, Cincinnati on October 23, and Pittsburgh on November 15**. Presales for those four started Tuesday, September 1 at 10 a.m. local time, and the general public on-sale is **Friday, September 4 at 10 a.m. local**. Every other 2026 and 2027 date is already on sale.

That brings the tour to 92 announced shows. **36 of them are still ahead**, starting with Montreal on September 2 and running through Vancouver on December 17, before the tour crosses into South America, the UK and Europe in 2027. There is no opening act on any date: these are billed as "evening with" shows, two full sets a night with no support slot.

These are single nights. Every other city on the North American run got two or more shows, which makes Houston, St. Louis, Cincinnati and Pittsburgh the four hardest tickets on the schedule by simple math.

## Who Is in the Band Now

This matters more than usual, because the lineup is not the one that ended the R40 Tour in 2015. **Geddy Lee** plays bass, keys and sings. **Alex Lifeson** plays guitar. **Anika Nilles**, the German drummer and composer who spent more than 60 shows behind Jeff Beck, takes the drum chair. **Loren Gold**, who has played with the Who and Roger Daltrey, was added on keyboards.

Neil Peart died in 2020, and the tour is framed around honoring him rather than replacing him. Peart's widow Carrie Nuttall-Peart and daughter Olivia gave the run their public support when it was announced.

## What the Show Is Actually Like

Two sets a night, no opener, roughly three hours. The band builds each night's setlist from a pool of more than 40 songs, so consecutive nights in the same city genuinely differ. If you are choosing between two nights in Boston, Cleveland or Denver, there is no wrong answer, and fans doing multiple nights are not paying twice for the same show.

Reviews from the summer leg have been strong across the board, with Rolling Stone, Billboard, Paste, Forbes and the Los Angeles Times all filing positive notices. Nilles in particular has drawn praise for handling a part that most people assumed was unplayable by anyone else.

## Tickets and Pricing

The practical picture as of September 1:

- **The four new cities are the only fresh primary inventory.** Everything else has been on sale for months, so what is left there is remaining primary plus resale.
- **Single-night markets carry a premium.** Houston, St. Louis, Cincinnati and Pittsburgh each get one show. Cities with two nights spread demand across both.
- **Midweek is the value.** The Monday in Boston, the Monday in Denver and the Monday in Tampa are the softest nights on the routing.
- **Watch for production holds.** Arena shows routinely release blocked seats behind and beside the stage in the final week before the show, and that is the legitimate way sold-out dates get seats back.
